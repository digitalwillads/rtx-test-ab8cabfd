Each bar fills at its real pace, with one minute of the test shown as one second here. DeepSeek finished the round in 2 minutes and Opus in 20. Part of that gap is speed, since DeepSeek writes about twice as fast. Most of it is length, because Opus wrote about five times more on a typical task, counting its thinking, while it checked its own work.

What this means for buying the box

Three things changed what we would buy, and one thing still needs a real-work trial.

The cheaper-to-cool card is enough

While writing, each card drew about 245 watts and never passed 285. The Max-Q version is capped at 300 watts, so it writes just as fast and is easier to cool when two cards sit side by side.

The host needs 384 GB of memory

The model keeps a third of its experts and its lookup tables in the computer's memory, about 220 GiB, and that memory cannot swap to disk. A 256 GB host would be too small.

Tool use is as clean as Opus

Neither model made a single broken tool call. With 151 Barbara tool names in front of it, DeepSeek still picked the right ones and never sent an email or applied a change it was told only to stage.

Hard work still needs a trial

Our tasks are short and clear. Public benchmarks still rank DeepSeek below Opus on long, hard work, so the next step is a day of real work with no client data on it.
